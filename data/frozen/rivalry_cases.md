## Antz (1998) / A Bug's Life (1998)

- **Films involved:** *Antz* (DreamWorks Animation) and *A Bug's Life* (Pixar Animation Studios).
- **Catalogue position:** *Antz* is `CORE_DWA`; *A Bug's Life* is `DISNEY_PIXAR`. Pixar remains the studio attribution and is not relabelled as WDAS.
- **Disney-Pixar basis:** Pixar SEC filings identify *A Bug's Life* as the first original film under the 1997 Disney-Pixar Co-Production Agreement.
- **Temporal/thematic relationship:** both are 1998 computer-animated ant-colony stories released within roughly two months.
- **Documented competitive relationship:** contemporary reporting discussed the parallel premises, accusations surrounding the projects, and release-date maneuvering.
- **Narrative use:** strongest early direct-rivalry case. It supports a Disney-side versus DreamWorks narrative without collapsing Pixar into WDAS.
- **Evidence strength:** high.
- **Partnership evidence:** https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1002114/000089161802001531/f80223e10-k405.htm
- **Contemporary rivalry evidence:** https://www.wired.com/1998/11/is-one-bug-as-good-as-another/

## The Road to El Dorado (2000) / The Emperor's New Groove (2000)

- **Films involved:** *The Road to El Dorado* (DreamWorks Animation) and *The Emperor's New Groove* (Walt Disney Animation Studios).
- **Catalogue position:** both remain core films; no scope change affects them.
- **Temporal relationship:** both were released in 2000.
- **Thematic relationship:** both are comic animated adventures drawing on stylized pre-Columbian American settings.
- **Documented relationship:** production-history reporting records that similarities were noticed during production and includes testimony describing a race to release; the evidence remains less direct than for *Antz* / *A Bug's Life*.
- **Narrative use:** cautious production-era comparison, not a quantitative rivalry pair.
- **Evidence strength:** moderate.
- **Production-history interview:** https://am.animatedviews.com/TheEmperorsNewGroove.html

## Shrek (2001)

- **Film involved:** *Shrek* (DreamWorks Animation).
- **Pairing:** no WDAS or Pixar counterpart is assigned.
- **Thematic relationship:** the film deliberately subverts familiar fairy-tale conventions.
- **Documented relationship:** contemporary Cannes reporting described the film as irreverent and subversive, noted that it had been interpreted as an attack on Disney, and recorded Jeffrey Katzenberg rejecting a personal-vendetta interpretation.
- **Narrative use:** supports a sourced discussion of DreamWorks differentiating itself through fairy-tale parody/subversion. It must not become a causal variable or invented one-to-one pair.
- **Evidence strength:** high for the subversion/Disney-context annotation.
- **Contemporary Cannes / non-vendetta evidence:** https://www.wired.com/2001/05/cannes-fairy-tales/
- **Contemporary Duloc / Disney-context evidence:** https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/lifestyle/2001/03/31/a-tale-of-two-studios/95dfada9-16f9-4052-a4d9-4f9d76dd222b/

## Anti-cherry-picking rule

These annotations exist only after the systematic catalogue has been defined. No film is included or excluded because it strengthens or weakens the three-part narrative.
